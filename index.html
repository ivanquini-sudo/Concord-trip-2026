🇺🇸 Bunker Hill Monument

Charlestown, Boston • Saturday
History

On June 17, 1775, New England militia fought British troops in the first major pitched battle of the American Revolution. Despite the famous name, the fiercest fighting took place on Breed's Hill. The British took the ground, but the losses they suffered shocked both sides and showed that colonial militia could fight a professional army.

Did you know?The monument is 221 feet 5 inches tall and has 294 steps to the top.
What to look forLook across the hill and imagine the open fields of 1775. The museum across the street helps explain the battle.
👵 Family note: You do not need to climb the monument to enjoy the site. The National Park Service lists benches/seating at the monument area.
Source: National Park Service

⚓ USS Constitution

Charlestown Navy Yard • Saturday
Old Ironsides

Congress authorized USS Constitution as one of six frigates in 1794. After two failed launch attempts, she finally entered Boston Harbor on October 21, 1797. Today she is the world's oldest commissioned warship afloat and still has an active-duty U.S. Navy crew.

Did you know?Her nickname, “Old Ironsides,” came from the War of 1812, when sailors saw British cannonballs appear to bounce off her wooden sides.
What to look forNotice the enormous masts, rigging and gun deck. Her 1812 sailing rig used 48 sails and more than an acre of canvas.
👵 Family note: The USS Constitution Museum, Navy Yard and visitor center are accessible. The historic ship itself has very limited accessibility, so the museum is the easier option for anyone avoiding ladders or steep steps.
Sources: USS Constitution Museum; Naval History and Heritage Command

🏮 Old North Church

Boston North End • Saturday
1775

This is the place behind the phrase “one if by land, two if by sea.” On April 18, 1775, sexton Robert Newman and vestryman John Pulling Jr. climbed the steeple and briefly displayed two lanterns. The signal told a network of riders across the river that British troops were moving by water.

Did you know?The lanterns were only one piece of a larger warning system. Paul Revere was one rider among many spreading the alarm.
What to look forLook up at the steeple and imagine two lanterns burning there for about a minute while Boston was under military curfew.
👵 Family note: The sanctuary has wheelchair access and an accessible restroom. Some historic spaces, including the steeple and balconies, still require stairs.
Source: Old North Illuminated

🏠 Paul Revere House

North Square, Boston • Saturday
c. 1680

Built around 1680, the Paul Revere House is downtown Boston's oldest building. Revere bought it in 1770. On the evening of April 18, 1775, he left this home before beginning the ride that became one of the best-known stories of the Revolution.

Did you know?Paul Revere was much more than a messenger. He was a skilled silversmith, businessman and civic figure with a long life after 1775.
What to look forNotice how small and intimate the rooms feel compared with modern homes. The building has survived for more than three centuries.
👵 Family note: The house is now fully accessible, including elevator access to the second floor, though the historic building still has narrow doorways, uneven floors and low light in places.
Source: Paul Revere Memorial Association

🍝 Boston's North End

Dinner stop • Saturday
Neighborhood

The North End ties your Saturday together. It contains Old North Church, the Paul Revere House and streets that preserve the scale of early Boston. Later generations transformed the neighborhood again, including the Italian-American community that helped make today's North End one of Boston's best-known dining districts.

Family challengeAs you walk to dinner, see who can spot the oldest-looking doorway, narrowest street, or best view of a church steeple.

🎡 Deerfield Fair

Deerfield, New Hampshire • Sunday option
Since 1876

The first Deerfield Fair was held in 1876 as a one-day community event. It grew over generations, moved to its present fairgrounds, and became a four-day fair in 1950. Today the grounds cover more than 100 acres.

Did you know?Early fairs featured oxen, horses, cattle, sheep, poultry, butter, cheese, fruit, grain, vegetables, flowers and needlework.
2026The fair runs October 1–4. Sunday hours are listed as 8 AM–7 PM.
👵 Family note: This is the highest-walking, highest-crowd option on Sunday. Treat it as a browse-and-rest day rather than trying to see everything.
Source: Deerfield Fair Association

🎃 Salem & Haunted Happenings

Salem, Massachusetts • Sunday option
October

Salem mixes its 17th-century history with a huge modern Halloween season. Salem Common, an eight-acre park that has been public common space since the 1600s, becomes one of the centers of activity during Haunted Happenings.

For your dateHaunted Biz Baz is scheduled for Sunday, Oct. 4, 10 AM–6 PM.
What to look forCostumes, street vendors and historic architecture all share the same downtown. It is part history trip and part October festival.
👵 Family note: October crowds can be intense. Pick one meeting point and keep the grandparents with a designated companion rather than splitting up.
Source: Destination Salem

🕯️ Witch Dungeon Museum

Salem • Sunday option
1692 story

The museum's experience begins with a live reenactment of the 1692 trial of Sarah Good, using material from the original trial transcript. A guided section then uses recreated scenes to tell the story of Salem Village and the witch trials.

Keep in mindThis is a theatrical museum interpretation of the 1692 events, so treat it as a storytelling experience rather than the original courtroom or jail itself.
Source: Witch Dungeon Museum

⛵ Portsmouth

New Hampshire Seacoast • Sunday dinner
Port city

Portsmouth was one of New England's major port cities in the 18th and early 19th centuries. Before independence it was the center of British political power in New Hampshire, and the city was deeply divided over the Revolution. Its maritime character is still visible in the working waterfront and compact historic downtown.

Did you know?New Hampshire's seacoast played an early role in resistance to Britain, including the 1774 raid on Fort William and Mary near Portsmouth.
What to look forHistoric brick buildings, narrow streets, harbor views and reminders that trade and shipbuilding once drove the region.
Sources: Visit New Hampshire; Portsmouth Historical Society

🏛️ New Hampshire State House

Concord • Monday
Free

The legislature moved into the State House in 1819. Its Representatives Hall is the oldest legislative chamber in the United States still in continuous use. New Hampshire's unusually large House has 400 members.

Did you know?There is no seat number 13 among the 400 seats in Representatives Hall.
What to look forThe chamber itself, portraits, flags and the feeling of being inside a working state capitol rather than a preserved museum.
👵 Family note: The official Visitor Center provides guided and self-guided State House tours and is open weekdays.
Source: New Hampshire General Court

🍎 Gould Hill Farm

Contoocook • Tuesday
Apples

Gould Hill has been farmed continuously since the mid-1700s. The property includes roughly 60 acres of orchards, fields and forest, and the main barn dates to 1810. Today the farm grows more than 80 apple varieties.

Did you know?By the 1920s, the farm's apples were moving through Boston markets and were eventually shipped around the world.
What to look forThe mountain views, old barn and the huge variety of apple shapes, colors and flavors.
Source: Gould Hill Farm

🍦 Ben & Jerry's Factory

Waterbury, Vermont • Tuesday
Ice cream

Waterbury is the only Ben & Jerry's factory open to the public. The factory was built in 1985 and can produce upwards of 350,000 pints per day. Tours started in 1986, while the company itself traces its retail beginning to a renovated gas station in Burlington in 1978.

Did you know?The outdoor Flavor Graveyard memorializes flavors that have been retired from production.
For your visitFall hours are listed as 10 AM–7 PM. Tickets are needed for the guided tour, but not for the retail area. Production is not guaranteed to be running during a visit.
👵 Family note: Restrooms are available outside along the paved walkway and inside the tour lobby.
Source: Ben & Jerry's official factory tour page
